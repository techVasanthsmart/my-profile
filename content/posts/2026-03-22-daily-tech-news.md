Below are concise tech items covering notable developments from 2026-03-21 to 2026-03-22.

1. Headline: Microsoft trims "unnecessary" Copilot entry points in Windows 11

Summary: Microsoft announced it is removing several Copilot buttons from Windows 11 apps including Snipping Tool, Photos, Widgets and Notepad to reduce clutter and improve reliability as part of broader Windows changes. The move is intended to streamline the user experience while keeping the core Copilot functionality.

Source: https://www.theverge.com (Tom Warren, Mar 20–21 coverage)

Companies/founders: Microsoft (Satya Nadella, CEO)

2. Headline: WordPress allows AI agents (e.g., ChatGPT, Claude) to draft and publish posts via MCP

Summary: WordPress.com introduced support for AI agents to draft blog posts through the Managed Publishing Connector (MCP); agent-written posts will arrive as drafts so users can review before publishing. The change formalizes third-party automation capability while keeping a human review step.

Source: TechCrunch / The Verge coverage (Mar 20)

Companies/founders: Automattic (Matt Mullenweg, CEO)

3. Headline: Samsung confirms Galaxy S26 will support AirDrop-like file sharing

Summary: Samsung said the Galaxy S26 will support AirDrop compatibility, expanding cross-device local file sharing beyond Apple devices — building on recent Android vendor efforts to standardize proximity sharing. The feature follows Google’s broader push to bring AirDrop-style functionality to more Android phones.

Source: The Verge (Choi Won-joon announcement via EBN, Mar 20)

Companies/founders: Samsung (Choi Won-joon, COO of Mobile eXperience Business)

4. Headline: Google Messages gets Trash folder and @-mentions for RCS group chats

Summary: Google updated Messages to add a Trash folder (deleted messages retained 30 days) and @-mention support for RCS group chats, improving message lifecycle and group notification controls. These are incremental but helpful quality-of-life improvements ahead of broader RCS adoption.

Source: The Verge (Jay Peters, Mar 20)

Companies/founders: Google (Sundar Pichai, CEO)

5. Headline: Nevada court bars Kalshi from operating pending licensing

Summary: A Nevada judge issued a temporary restraining order preventing prediction markets operator Kalshi from operating in the state without a gaming license, intensifying regulatory friction between states and federal agencies over financial/prediction market oversight.

Source: The Verge (Sheena Vasani, Mar 20)

Companies/founders: Kalshi (founders: Jacob Klein and others)

6. Headline: Pentagon restrictions on journalists ruled unconstitutional

Summary: A federal court found recent Pentagon restrictions on journalists unconstitutional, a decision with potential implications for press access policies around defense reporting and for how government agencies structure media rules.

Source: The Verge (Jay Peters, Mar 20)

Companies/founders: U.S. Department of Defense (policy context)

What this means

- Big platform UI changes keep arriving: Microsoft’s pullback on Copilot entry points shows UX and reliability trade-offs are still shaping generative-AI integrations.
- AI automation is being productized: WordPress enabling agent-driven drafts formalizes publishers’ adoption of generative agents while retaining human-in-the-loop safeguards.
- Cross‑platform convenience continues: AirDrop-style interoperability on Android signals stronger vendor cooperation around core mobile UX features.
- Incremental improvements matter: Small Messaging updates (Trash, @mentions) are the kind of friction removals that nudge RCS forward.
- Regulation and media oversight remain active flashpoints: Kalshi’s injunction and the Pentagon ruling both demonstrate that legal and policy outcomes will continue to reshape markets and public-interest reporting.
